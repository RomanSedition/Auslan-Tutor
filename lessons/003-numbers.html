Numbers 0–19

Numbers come up constantly: ages, phone numbers, prices, times, dates. The good news is that Auslan numbers follow patterns, so once you see the logic, 0–19 falls into place quickly.

By the end you can…

  • Sign and recognise 0–19
  • Use the thumb pattern for 6–9
  • Ask someone's age and say yours
  • Give a phone number digit by digit

2 · Six to nine: the thumb trick

Here Auslan does something clever. SIX is just the thumb. Then add fingers: thumb + 1 finger = SEVEN, thumb + 2 = EIGHT, thumb + 3 = NINE. Think “five on the thumb, plus whatever's showing”.

Just the thumb, pointing up. (6–9 are built on the thumb.)

Thumb plus index finger, index pointing sideways.

Thumb plus two fingers, pointing sideways.

Thumb plus three fingers, pointing sideways.
Don't mix up SIX and GOOD

SIX (thumb) looks a lot like the thumbs-up GOOD you learned in Lesson 2. Context sorts it out: in a list of numbers or after AGE, it's six. Watch the palm orientation in each video, and how the hand is held.

4 · Eleven to nineteen

For 13–19, the handshape is the same as 3–9, with an added movement that turns it into a “teen”. Compare THREE with THIRTEEN, and SEVEN with SEVENTEEN, at 0.5× to see the difference. ELEVEN and TWELVE are their own signs, and have regional versions. Use the Regional versions chips to compare.

5 · Using numbers

Hand in front of the nose, closing as it moves down. With raised eyebrows: 'How old?'

Bunched fingertips at the mouth with a small movement.

Thumb and little finger out (an old handset) held to the ear.
Phone numbers

Sign phone numbers one digit at a time, keeping a steady rhythm. Pause slightly between groups (like the spaces in 0412 345 678). It's fine to check by writing it down or texting it. Deaf people do that too!

Asking someone's age

AGE on its own, with a questioning face, asks “How old?”. Because it's a wh-question (how old), use the eyebrows-down face from Lesson 2. To answer, sign AGE then the number.

Deaf culture: Numbers vary by region

Numbers are one of the places you'll notice regional differences in Auslan, as with ELEVEN and TWELVE here. Signbank also notes an old regional SIX that's becoming rare. If you set your state on the home page, you'll see your region's versions first.

Put it together

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror and R to record a take, then compare it side by side with the reference.

  1. Count up and down. 0 → 10 → 0, holding each number briefly. Then try it faster.
  2. The thumb series. FIVE, SIX, SEVEN, EIGHT, NINE. Feel how 6–9 restart on the thumb.
  3. Teens vs ones. Record THREE then THIRTEEN, and SEVEN then SEVENTEEN, and compare the movement at 0.5×.
  4. Your phone number. Sign it digit by digit, three times, with even rhythm.
  5. Your age. Sign YOU AGE? (eyebrows down), then answer ME AGE …. Use 1–19 for now; bigger numbers come in Lesson 10.

Check yourself

Recap

0–5 count on your fingers. 6–9 start again from the thumb. 13–19 use the 3–9 handshape plus a movement, while 11 and 12 are their own signs (with regional versions). Phone numbers go digit by digit; ask age with AGE and eyebrows down.
