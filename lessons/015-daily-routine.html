Lesson 15Everyday~35 min

Daily routine

Describe a normal day from waking up to going to bed. Combine these signs with Lesson 13's time-first order and you can talk about your day, your week, and what you did yesterday.

By the end you can…

  • Sign everyday actions: EAT, DRINK, WORK, GO, COME, SLEEP
  • Sign meals and parts of the day
  • Describe your day in order using time-first
  • Ask someone about their day

1 · Things you do

Hand above the head, fingers flicking down: water spraying.

Bunched fingertips move to the mouth: putting food in.

Curved hand like holding a cup, tipped to the mouth.

Flat hand taps the edge of the other hand repeatedly.

Open hand raised, then moves away from you.

Index finger beckons towards you.

Starts as a closed hand near the shoulder, then the open hand comes down. Watch at 0.5×.

Thumb and fingers close in front of the eye: eyes closing.
GO and COME have direction

GO moves away from you, towards where you're going. COME moves towards you. You can aim GO at a place you've set up in space: (point left = work) ME GO (towards the left).

2 · Meals

Australia-wide: thumbs-up, then flat hand slides over the other: GOOD-MORNING + EAT. Southern: from the nose to a thumbs-up.

Edge of the flat hand taps under the chin.

Southern: hand at the chin moves down. Northern: the EAT sign. NSW: knife-and-fork movements.

BREAKFAST is literally GOOD-MORNING + EAT in the Australia-wide version. DINNER has regional versions; the Northern one is the same as EAT.

3 · Parts of the day

Thumbs-up (GOOD) hand at the upper chest. The Northern and Southern versions differ slightly, so compare them at 0.25×.

Five regional versions! SA: hand under the chin. Victoria: index near the chin. Tasmania: flat hand under the chin.

Australia-wide: both flat hands cross in front of the body, closing in like darkness. Southern: V-hand at the eyes.

AFTERNOON has five versions: SA, Victoria, Tasmania, Queensland and Northern. GOOD MORNING is back from Lesson 2.

Deaf culture: Visual alarms

Many Deaf people use vibrating alarm clocks, flashing doorbells and baby monitors, and phone alerts that vibrate or flash. A Deaf person's 'daily routine' includes a lot of clever visual and tactile tech.

Put it together

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror and R to record a take, then compare it side by side with the reference.

  1. Actions. EAT, DRINK, WORK, GO, COME, SLEEP: five times each, clean and clear.
  2. Meals. BREAKFAST, LUNCH, DINNER in your region's versions.
  3. Your day, in order. GOOD-MORNING … AFTERNOON … NIGHT …: at least five steps.
  4. Yesterday. Retell your day with YESTERDAY at the start and FINISH at the end.
  5. Record it (12 s) and check the time words come first.

Check yourself

Recap

Actions + time words = your whole day. GO moves away, COME moves towards you. Meals and parts of the day have regional versions, so use the chips.
