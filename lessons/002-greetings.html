Greetings & introductions

Your first real conversation: say hello, ask how someone is, swap names, say you're learning Auslan, and say goodbye. You'll also meet the most important idea in Auslan grammar: your face is part of the sentence.

By the end you can…

  • Greet someone and ask “How are you?”, and answer it
  • Say “My name is ” and ask “What's your name?”
  • Say “Nice to meet you”, “Thank you” and “See you later”
  • Tell someone “I'm hearing, I'm learning Auslan”
  • Use raised and lowered eyebrows to mark the two kinds of question

Good morning: a regional sign

How it works

Your eyebrows are grammar

English marks questions with word order and tone of voice. Auslan uses your face:

  • Yes/no questions (“Are you Deaf?”): eyebrows raised, head tilted slightly forward, and hold the look until the last sign.
  • Wh- questions (what, who, where, how): eyebrows down, a slight frown.

That's why GOOD and HOW ARE YOU? can be the same hand. A sign without the right face can read as a different sentence.

Auslan isn't English on the hands

Auslan has its own word order. Small English words like is, are, to, the usually have no sign. Question signs like WHAT often go at the end: “What's your name?” becomes YOUR NAME WHAT?. The glosses below (sign words in CAPITALS) show the Auslan order.

Deaf culture: eye contact & getting attention

Keep eye contact while someone is signing to you. Looking away is like putting your fingers in your ears mid-sentence. To get a Deaf person's attention, wave in their field of view or tap them lightly on the shoulder or arm.

Many Deaf people write Deaf with a capital D to mean being part of the Deaf community and its culture and language, not just “can't hear”. Telling people you're hearing and learning Auslan is welcome. People will usually slow down and help.

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror and R to record. This lesson is as much about your face as your hands, so check your eyebrows in each take.

  1. Face warm-up. In the mirror, raise your eyebrows (yes/no question), then lower them (wh- question). Five each, and make them big. It'll feel exaggerated, but it isn't.
  2. GOOD vs HOW ARE YOU? Sign GOOD with a nod. Then the same hand with eyebrows up. Record both and check that they look like a statement and a question.
  3. Introduce yourself. HELLO → MY NAME → ME HEARING → ME LEARN AUSLAN. Record it as one take (8 s) and compare against the phrase videos.
  4. Ask questions back. YOUR NAME WHAT? (eyebrows down) · YOU DEAF? (eyebrows up). Watch the eyebrows on playback.
  5. Close the conversation. NICE MEET YOU → THANK-YOU → SEE-YOU-AGAIN → BYE.

Role-play: both sides

A: HELLO! HOW-ARE-YOU?  (eyebrows up)
B: GOOD, THANK-YOU. YOU?  (nod, then eyebrows up)
A: GOOD. YOUR NAME WHAT?  (eyebrows down)
B: MY NAME . YOUR NAME WHAT?
A: (fingerspells a name)
B: NICE MEET YOU. ME HEARING. ME LEARN AUSLAN.
A: GOOD!  (big nod)
B: THANK-YOU. SEE-YOU-AGAIN. BYE!

Sign both parts in the mirror. Make up a name for A and fingerspell it.

Recap

You can now hold a whole first-meeting conversation. The key ideas:

  • Pointing is grammar: ME, YOU, and a fist for MY/YOUR.
  • Eyebrows up for yes/no questions, eyebrows down for wh- questions.
  • Question signs go at the end: YOUR NAME WHAT?
  • Some signs are regional. Learn the version used where you live.
