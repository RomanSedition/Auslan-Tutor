Days of the week & clock time

Days of the week are mostly Australia-wide, and several come from fingerspelling: TUESDAY is a T, WEEK/WEDNESDAY is a W, FRIDAY is an F. Spotting the letters makes them much easier to remember.

By the end you can…

  • Sign all seven days
  • Spot which days come from fingerspelled letters
  • Ask WHAT TIME? and give a time with numbers
  • Talk about the weekend

1 · The week

Hand moves across the front of the other upright hand. Watch at 0.5×.

Index finger touches the side of the other upright hand: the letter T. (TOILET uses the same sign.)

Fingers of both hands interlock: the letter W. WEEK and WEDNESDAY share this sign.

Both flat hands, bent at the knuckles, with a small movement side by side. Watch at 0.5×.

The letter F (two fingers across two fingers) tapped.

Bent fingers (a claw) at the chin, moving forward.

Both flat hands come together, palms touching, as if praying.
Letters hiding in the days

TUESDAY is the letter T on the hand, FRIDAY is an F, and WEEK and WEDNESDAY share one sign: interlocked fingers, the letter W. Context tells you which is meant: NEXT WEEK versus ON WEDNESDAY.

2 · Weekend and time

Both hands with crossed fingers near the chest, moving down. Watch at 0.5×.

Index finger taps the back of the other wrist: where a watch goes.

Flat hand circles in front of the face, then rests on the forehead. Watch at 0.5×.

Index finger moves a short way along the other hand: a small step on a clock.
Telling the time

Tap the wrist (TIME), then sign the number: TIME THREE = 3 o'clock. To ask, sign TIME WHAT? or tap the wrist with a questioning face (eyebrows down). For exact times, many people just write it or show a phone.

Deaf culture: Planning ahead

Deaf people often plan meetups precisely (day, time and place) because you can't just call out across a room or ring someone at the last minute the way hearing people do. Being on time and confirming plans by text is appreciated.

Put it together

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror and R to record a take, then compare it side by side with the reference.

  1. Days in order. Monday to Sunday, twice. Then backwards.
  2. Spot the letters. Fingerspell T then TUESDAY, W then WEEK, F then FRIDAY.
  3. Your week. For each day, sign one thing you do: MONDAY WORK, TUESDAY …
  4. Times. Sign TIME + five different hours. Then ask TIME WHAT? with the right face.
  5. Record your weekend plan: SATURDAY …, SUNDAY …

Check yourself

Recap

Several days come from letters: TUESDAY (T), WEEK/WEDNESDAY (W), FRIDAY (F). Time is TIME + number. Ask with TIME WHAT? and eyebrows down.
