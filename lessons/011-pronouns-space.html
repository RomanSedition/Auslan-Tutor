Pronouns & signing space

This is one of the most important grammar ideas in Auslan. Instead of saying “he” or “she”, you place people in the space in front of you and then point back to them. It's precise, efficient, and once it clicks it feels natural.

By the end you can…

  • Set up people at locations in space and refer back to them
  • Use HE/SHE, WE, THEY by pointing
  • Show possession: MY, YOUR, OUR, THEIR (and his/her by direction)
  • Use BOTH, ALL and EVERYONE

1 · Pointing pronouns

Point to where the person is, or to the spot you've set up for them.

Index sweeps around to include you and the people with you.

Index sweeps across the places where those people are.
One sign for he, she and it

Auslan's pointing pronoun doesn't mark gender: the same point means he, she, it or there. You know who it is because of where you point. If you need to say someone's gender, you sign it separately.

2 · Possession

MY-style fist moves in an arc across the chest.

YOUR-style fist aimed at, and sweeping across, their location.
His and her: aim the fist

You know MY (fist on your chest) and YOUR (fist toward the person). For his/her, use the same fist aimed at that person's location. Signbank's video for HIS isn't currently working, so practise this as a pattern: the direction tells you whose it is.

3 · Groups

Two-finger (V) hand moves between the two people.

Flat hand, palm down, sweeps around in a circle.

Both index fingers up, sweeping round to everyone. Watch at 0.5×.

How signing space works

Set up, then point back
  1. Introduce a person and place them: MY SISTER → point to a spot on your left.
  2. Introduce another: MY BROTHER → point to your right.
  3. Now just point left or right to mean her or him: (left) DEAF, (right) HEARING = she's Deaf, he's hearing.
  4. Keep the spots consistent for the whole conversation.

If the person is actually present, just point at them.

Deaf culture: Pointing is polite in Auslan

Pointing at people can feel rude in English-speaking culture, but in Auslan it's ordinary grammar. Not pointing can actually make you harder to understand.

Put it together

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror and R to record a take, then compare it side by side with the reference.

  1. Set up two people. MY SISTER → place her left. MY FATHER → place him right. Then point: (left) TALL, (right) SHORT.
  2. Possession by direction. Aim the MY/YOUR fist at the left spot for “her” and the right spot for “his”: (left)-FIST HAIR RED.
  3. WE vs THEY. Sign WE LEARN AUSLAN, then THEY DEAF. Check that WE includes you and THEY sweeps over there.
  4. BOTH and ALL. BOTH DEAF (about your two spots) · EVERYONE HAPPY.
  5. Record a mini story (8–12 s): set up two family members, describe each, and say how they feel. Watch that your spots stay put.

Check yourself

Recap

Place people in space, then point back. One pointing sign covers he/she/it. Possessive fists aim at people's locations. WE includes you; THEY sweeps across others; BOTH, ALL and EVERYONE group them.
