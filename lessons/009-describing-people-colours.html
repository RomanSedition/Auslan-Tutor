Describing people & colours

Describe someone so another person could spot them in a crowd. Along the way you'll meet colours, which are one of the most regionally varied parts of Auslan, so this lesson uses the regional chips a lot.

By the end you can…

  • Describe someone's height, age, hair and features
  • Sign 12 colours in your region's version
  • Recognise the other regional versions when you see them
  • Put descriptions in a natural Auslan order

Signbank uses the same video for the Australia-wide GREY and the Southern PURPLE (flat hand on the cheek). In the Southern region that one sign can mean either colour, so you rely on context. If it matters, add a fingerspelled word or point to something that colour.

How it works

Description order

Auslan often puts the thing first and the description after: HAIR RED, MAN TALL. When describing a person, a natural order is: general (tall/short, old/young) → hair → face (glasses, beard) → clothes. Point to the person (or their spot) to keep it clear who you mean.

Deaf culture: Describing people is normal and expected

Deaf people often describe others very directly and visually, including height, hair, glasses, even weight or distinctive features, because visual description is how you identify someone in a visual language. It isn't rude in the way it might feel in English. Sign names often come from exactly these features.

Put it together

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror and R to record a take, then compare it side by side with the reference.

  1. Colour run. Sign all 12 colours in your region's version. Then use the chips to look at one other region's RED, BLUE and YELLOW.
  2. Describe yourself. Height, hair colour, glasses or beard, in that order.
  3. Describe someone else. Pick a family member: point to a spot, then describe them.
  4. Look-alikes. Record OLD and AGE and compare. Then click through the PURPLE and GREY chips to see which regions share a sign.
  5. Spot the person. Describe someone in a photo on your phone as if a friend had to find them.

Check yourself

Recap

Description usually follows the thing: HAIR RED. Colours vary by region: learn yours, recognise the others. Direct visual description of people is normal in Auslan.
