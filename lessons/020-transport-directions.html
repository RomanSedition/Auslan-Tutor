Transport & directions

Getting around: how you travel, and how to give and follow directions. Directions are where signing space really shines, because you can show the route rather than describe it.

By the end you can…

  • Sign the main kinds of transport
  • Give simple directions: STRAIGHT, LEFT, RIGHT
  • Say if something is NEAR or FAR
  • Ask how someone gets somewhere

Show the route

For directions, imagine you're travelling from where you are: STRAIGHT (hand moves forward), LEFT (hand turns left), then SHOP THERE (point). Your hands draw the map in front of you, from the point of view of the person going.

FAR and your face

The further away something is, the bigger and slower the movement, and your face shows it: squinting eyes or puffed cheeks for 'really far'. A small quick movement with tight lips means 'close by'.

Deaf culture: Transport access

Train and bus announcements are often audio-only, which can leave Deaf travellers missing platform changes and delays. Visual displays, captions and apps make a big difference.

Put it together

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror and R to record a take, then compare it side by side with the reference.

  1. Transport. All seven, then: ME GO WORK (how?).
  2. Directions. Give directions from your front door to the nearest shop: STRAIGHT, LEFT/RIGHT, NEAR/FAR.
  3. Near vs far. Sign BEACH NEAR, then BEACH FAR VERY: change the movement and face.
  4. Ask. YOU GO HOW? · TOILET WHERE? and follow your own directions.
  5. Record a route and watch it back. Could someone follow it?
