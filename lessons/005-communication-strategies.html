Communication strategies

As a learner you will miss things, constantly. That's normal. The skill that makes conversations work is knowing how to ask for help: to repeat, to slow down, to fingerspell, or to ask what a sign means.

By the end you can…

  • Ask someone to repeat or slow down
  • Say you do or don't understand (and be honest about it)
  • Ask for fingerspelling or for the sign for a word
  • Apologise, say please, ask someone to wait, and get attention politely

“I don't understand” with a headshake

To say you don't understand, sign ME UNDERSTAND with a clear headshake and a puzzled face. The headshake makes it negative. (There are other negative signs; you'll meet them in Lesson 17.)

2 · Being polite

Australia-wide: curved hand at the chin with a small movement and a sorry face.

Southern: open hand moves out from the chest. Northern: flat hand from the mouth ends as a thumbs-up.

Fingertips of one hand brush across the other palm.

Index finger held up: 'hang on a moment'.
PLEASE and THANK-YOU

In the Southern region, Signbank shows PLEASE with the same sign as THANK-YOU (Lesson 2): flat hand moving out from the chin. Context and your face tell people which you mean. The Northern PLEASE is a different sign.

SORRY and PLEASE have regional versions. Your region's version is shown first, and the chips let you compare.

How it works

Asking “how do you sign…?”

Fingerspell the English word, then sign SIGN HOW? (eyebrows down): E-M-U, SIGN HOW?. Or point at the thing and ask SIGN WHAT?. People are usually happy to show you.

Deaf culture: Don't fake it

It's tempting to nod along when you're lost, but Deaf people will usually spot it, and it causes real misunderstandings. Saying AGAIN PLEASE or SLOW PLEASE is completely normal and respected. Writing a word down, or typing it on your phone, is a fine backup too.

Deaf culture: Getting attention

To get a Deaf person's attention: wave in their line of sight, lightly tap their shoulder or upper arm, or (in a group) ask someone nearby to pass it on. Flicking the lights is used for a whole room. Stamping or banging the table is used in some situations, but read the room first. EXCUSE-ME is for squeezing past or stepping away from a conversation.

Put it together

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror and R to record a take, then compare it side by side with the reference.

  1. The rescue kit. AGAIN PLEASE · SLOW PLEASE · FINGERSPELL PLEASE. Five times each until they're automatic.
  2. Headshake negation. ME UNDERSTAND with a nod (I understand), then with a headshake (I don't). Record both.
  3. How do you sign…? Fingerspell three words (try E-M-U, K-I-W-I, B-E-A-C-H), each followed by SIGN HOW?
  4. Apologise and wait. SORRY with a genuine sorry face, then WAIT. Compare your face with the video.
  5. Role-play. Imagine someone signs too fast: AGAIN PLEASE → SLOW PLEASE → (nod) UNDERSTAND, THANK-YOU.

Check yourself

Recap

You now have a rescue kit: AGAIN, SLOW, FINGERSPELL, SIGN HOW?, WAIT, and honest UNDERSTAND with a nod or headshake. Using these is a sign of a good learner, not a weak one.
