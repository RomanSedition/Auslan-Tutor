Role shift: becoming the character

Role shift (also called constructed action) is how Auslan quotes people and shows their reactions. Instead of saying 'he said…', you shift your shoulders and gaze, and become him. It's the key to storytelling.

By the end you can…

  • Shift your body and gaze to become a character
  • Quote two people in a conversation
  • Show reactions: surprised, shocked, laughing, crying
  • Use LOOK, SEE, THINK in a story

How role shift works

Shift, look, become
  1. Introduce the characters and give each a spot: MY MOTHER (left), MY SISTER (right).
  2. To become your mother, turn your shoulders slightly and look towards your sister's spot, as your mother would.
  3. Now everything you sign is your mother speaking, including her facial expression.
  4. To switch to your sister, turn the other way and look back.

You're not narrating; you're acting it out.

Reactions carry the story

Role shift is great for reactions: show SURPRISED or SHOCKED as the character, with their face. Often the reaction alone, with no sign at all, tells the story.

Deaf culture: Deaf humour

A lot of Deaf humour depends on role shift and visual exaggeration: impersonating people, acting out awkward moments, and especially jokes about hearing people's assumptions. Being able to follow role shift opens up a lot of Deaf humour.

Put it together

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror and R to record a take, then compare it side by side with the reference.

  1. Two spots. Set up FRIEND (left) and BOSS (right). Shift to look at each.
  2. Quote a dialogue. Friend: YOU COME SATURDAY? → You: ME BUSY. Shift between roles.
  3. Reactions. Become someone who's SURPRISED, then SHOCKED, then LAUGHING. Face first.
  4. Mini story. Your friend SEES a dog, is SHOCKED, then LAUGHS. Narrate, then role shift.
  5. Record and check: is it clear who's talking at each moment?

Recap

Role shift: turn your shoulders and gaze to become a character. Their face is your face. It's how Auslan quotes people and tells stories.
