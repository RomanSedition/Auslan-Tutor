Lesson 8People~30 min

Feelings & emotions

Feelings are where your face matters most. In Auslan the facial expression isn't decoration; it carries the meaning and how strong it is. A bit tired and completely exhausted can use the same handshape, with very different faces and movement.

By the end you can…

  • Sign 10+ feelings with a matching face
  • Show intensity (a bit / very) with face and movement
  • Answer HOW-ARE-YOU? with more than GOOD
  • Ask how someone feels and why

1 · Everyday feelings

Flat hand brushes up the other palm repeatedly, with a smile.

Index finger drawn down from the mouth, with a sad face.

Bent hand at the chest drops down; tired face, slumped shoulders.

Touch the forehead, then the hands come together in front of the body.

Thumb-and-finger ring at the chin flicks out; bored face.

Open hand with a small side-to-side wobble: 'fine, okay'.

2 · Strong feelings

Hand flicks out to the side with an angry face.

Both hands brush up the chest alternately; bright face.

Hand at the side of the forehead with a small, repeated movement; worried face.

Flat hand on the chest; frightened face.

Arms crossed over the chest: a hug.

3 · Talking about feelings

Bent middle finger brushes up the chest.

Australia-wide: both hands with a strong, tense movement. Southern: from the chin. Puff your cheeks for emphasis.
Intensity lives in the face and the movement

To make a feeling stronger you can add VERY, but more often signers change how they sign it: a bigger, slower or tenser movement, plus a stronger facial expression (puffed cheeks, squeezed eyes, raised shoulders). A small movement with a mild face = “a bit”. Try TIRED both ways in the mirror.

The face must match

Signing HAPPY with a blank face looks odd, or even sarcastic. Signing SAD with a smile is confusing. Let your face do what the sign says.

Deaf culture: Faces aren't “too much”

Hearing learners often feel self-conscious about big facial expressions. In Auslan they're simply grammar and vocabulary. What looks exaggerated in English conversation looks natural in Auslan. Watch Deaf signers, and copy them.

Put it together

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror and R to record a take, then compare it side by side with the reference.

  1. Face only. Without hands, make the face for each feeling: happy, sad, tired, angry, excited, worried, scared, bored. Record it. Could someone guess them?
  2. Now add hands. Sign each feeling with its face.
  3. Dial it up. Sign TIRED three ways: a bit tired, tired, exhausted. Change movement size, speed and face.
  4. Answer properly. HOW-ARE-YOU? Answer three different ways (e.g. ME TIRED · ME HAPPY · ME OKAY).
  5. Record & compare. Pick EXCITED or WORRIED and compare face and hands with the reference at 0.5×.

Check yourself

Recap

Feelings = hands + matching face. Intensity comes from movement size and face, with VERY as an extra. Now you can answer HOW-ARE-YOU? honestly.
