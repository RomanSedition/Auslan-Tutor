The two-handed alphabet

Fingerspelling is your safety net. Any word you don't know the sign for, any name, any place, you can spell it. Auslan uses a two-handed alphabet, so the passive hand works like a little whiteboard the other hand points at.

By the end you can…

  • Make all 26 letters, starting with the five vowels
  • Fingerspell your own name smoothly:
  • Read short fingerspelled words at slow speed

Before you start

Which hand does what?

Your dominant hand (right if you're right-handed) does the pointing and moving. Your passive hand is the base: mostly an open hand, palm towards you, fingers up or angled. Pick a dominant hand and stick with it. Swapping hands mid-word is like changing handwriting halfway through.

Read the word, not the letters

Fluent signers spell with a smooth rhythm, and readers pick up the overall shape of the word. Keep your hands in one comfortable spot in front of your chest, don't bounce between letters, and mouth the whole word rather than each letter.

1 · The vowels: point to your fingertips

This is the big trick. Spread your passive hand and touch the fingertips in order: thumb = A, index = E, middle = I, ring = O, little = U. Learn these five first, because almost every word uses them.

💡 A-E-I-O-U runs thumb → pinky in the same order you'd say them. Count them off: A, E, I, O, U.

2 · Fingers on the palm

Lay fingers flat on the passive palm. Count the fingers: one finger = L, two = N, three = M (like the number of humps in lowercase n and m). A V-shape = V; a crooked index = R.

Deaf culture: names and sign names

When you introduce yourself you'll fingerspell your name. Deaf people often have a sign name too, a short sign that refers to them. Sign names are given by members of the Deaf community, usually once they know you. You don't invent your own, so until then fingerspelling your name is exactly right.

Same family as British and NZ sign

Auslan's two-handed alphabet is shared (with small differences) with British Sign Language and New Zealand Sign Language. All three come from the same historical family, sometimes called BANZSL. American Sign Language uses a completely different one-handed alphabet, so beware of ASL fingerspelling charts online.

Read it & spell it

Read it: watch a short word and type what you see (start at 0.75×). Spell any word: type a word and watch every letter in a row. Try your name, your street, your pet.

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror. Press R to record a take, which plays back next to the reference so you can compare.

  1. Vowel warm-up. A-E-I-O-U five times, slowly, then five times quicker. Watch that your pointing finger lands right on each fingertip.
  2. Your name. Spell five times. Go for a steady rhythm with no pauses between letters. Use Spell any word above to check it.
  3. Three-letter words. CAT, DOG, SUN, BUS, CUP. Say each word on your lips while you spell it.
  4. Record & compare. Click the 🪞 button on a tricky letter (try K, P or Q), press R, sign it, then compare. Use the 0.5× speed for both.
  5. Read back. Do three rounds of Read it without slowing down.

Check yourself

Recap

Vowels are fingertips (thumb → pinky = A E I O U). Fingers on the palm count: L 1, N 2, M 3. Many letters draw their own shape (C, D, T, X). Spell with an even rhythm and mouth the whole word.
