Questions & facial expression

In Lesson 2 you met the idea that your eyebrows are grammar. This lesson goes deeper: the question words of Auslan, where they go in a sentence, and how your face, head and body turn a statement into a question.

By the end you can…

  • Sign the wh-question words: WHO, WHERE, WHEN, WHY, HOW, WHICH, HOW-MANY
  • Use the wh-question face (brows down) and the yes/no face (brows up) every time
  • Answer with YES and NO, plus a nod or headshake
  • Ask questions using signs from Lessons 1–3

1 · The question words

Every one of these is a wh-question, so every one takes the eyebrows-down face: a slight frown, head tilted a little forward, eye contact held until the end of the question.

Upright index finger with a small movement; eyebrows down.

Both hands palm-up, moving out to the sides; eyebrows down.

Fingers against the cheek, fluttering as if counting; eyebrows down.

Flat hand, palm down; the fingers bend down. Eyebrows down.

Southern: the backs of bent fingers roll against each other. Northern: hands open out, palms up.

Thumb and little finger out, rocking side to side between choices.

Open hand, fingers fluttering as if counting; eyebrows down.
WHY and BECAUSE

The same sign means WHY with a question face, and BECAUSE (or “the reason is…”) without one. Your face tells the other person which you mean.

HOW has regional versions

The card shows your region's HOW first. Don't confuse HOW with HOW-ARE-YOU from Lesson 2, which is the thumbs-up GOOD sign with a question face.

2 · Yes, no, question

A nodding hand (see the video), and nod your head too.

Fingers snap shut onto the thumb; shake your head.

Index finger moves against the raised fingers of the other hand. Watch at 0.5×.

In conversation, a lot of yes/no answering happens on the head: a nod means yes and a headshake means no, often with the sign as well. A headshake while you sign something also turns it negative. You'll use that more in Lesson 17.

How questions work

Two question faces, again
  • Yes/no questions (“Are you Deaf?”): eyebrows UP, eyes wide, head forward. Hold the face to the last sign.
  • Wh- questions (who, what, where, when, why, how, which, how many): eyebrows DOWN, slight frown.

If you only remember one thing from this lesson: the face is not optional. Without it, a question can look like a statement.

Where the question word goes

Auslan question signs very often go at the end of the question, and sometimes at both the start and the end: YOUR NAME WHAT? or WHAT YOUR NAME WHAT?. Signbank's entries for WHEN, WHY and HOW say exactly this. Putting it at the end is a safe default.

Deaf culture: Questions are direct, and that's fine

Auslan conversation can feel more direct than English. Short, clear questions with an obvious question face are normal and polite. Long, hedged English-style questions (“I was just wondering if maybe…”) don't translate well; ask simply and let your face do the softening.

Put it together

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror and R to record a take, then compare it side by side with the reference.

  1. Face drill. Sign WHO, WHERE, WHEN, WHY, HOW, WHICH, HOW-MANY, each with a deliberate eyebrows-down face. Record it and check that your face is doing the work.
  2. Flip the face. Sign YOU DEAF with brows up (a question), then the same signs with a nod (a statement: “you're Deaf”).
  3. Question at the end. YOUR NAME WHAT? · YOU LEARN AUSLAN WHY? · YOU AGE? Hold the face through the whole question.
  4. Answer yourself. Ask each question, then answer it: YES / NO with a nod or headshake, then a short answer.
  5. Record & compare. Pick WHEN or HOW-MANY, press R, and compare your hand and face to the reference.

Check yourself

Recap

Wh-questions: brows down. Yes/no questions: brows up. Question words usually go at the end. Nod for yes, shake your head for no, and keep eye contact while you ask.
