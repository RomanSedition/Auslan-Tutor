Negation: not, never, can't

You've been using the headshake since Lesson 5. Now it gets a proper look, alongside the negative signs. In Auslan, the headshake is often the main way to say 'not', and the signs add emphasis or a specific meaning.

By the end you can…

  • Make any sentence negative with a headshake
  • Use NOT, NOTHING, NEVER and CAN'T
  • Contrast CAN and CAN'T
  • Politely refuse with NO-THANKS

1 · Negative signs

Thumb and index ring flicks out from near the chin. Shake your head as well.

Open hand pushes out, palm forward: 'no way, no thanks'.

Thumb and index ring held up, a small flick: 'zero, nothing'. Watch at 0.5×.

Flat hand with a sharp downward chop beside the other hand; firm headshake.

2 · Can and can't

Bent index finger at the forehead or nose moves down to a fist. Watch at 0.5×.

Index finger at the lips, then up and out with a headshake.

How it works

The headshake carries it

Start the headshake with the part of the sentence you're negating and keep it going to the end: ME UNDERSTAND + headshake = I don't understand. A sign like NOT adds emphasis, but often the headshake alone is enough.

Where the negative goes

Negative signs often come at the end: ME LIKE MEAT NOT, ME GO NEVER. That's normal Auslan order, not a mistake.

Deaf culture: 'Can' and access

CAN and CAN'T come up a lot in Deaf people's lives: can't hear the announcement, can't use the phone, can understand with captions. Deaf people generally see access barriers as the problem, not deafness itself.

Recap

The headshake makes a sentence negative; NOT, NEVER, NOTHING and CAN'T add meaning or emphasis, often at the end.
