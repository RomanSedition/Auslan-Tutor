Directional verbs

Unit 5 is about Auslan's grammar in space. In English, word order shows who did what to whom ('I gave you' vs 'you gave me'). In Auslan, many verbs simply move from the giver to the receiver. One sign, aimed differently, gives you a whole sentence.

By the end you can…

  • Move directional verbs between people and spots
  • Sign I-GIVE-YOU vs YOU-GIVE-ME
  • Use set-up spots as the start and end of verbs
  • Tell a short exchange using directional verbs

1 · Directional verbs

Both hands move forward, palms up. The direction shows who gives to whom.

Hand from the chin moves towards the person you're asking. Directional.

Index from the lips moves towards the person you're telling. Directional.

Flat hand supports and lifts the other forearm. Direct it towards whoever's being helped.

Index finger points to the other palm, then moves towards the viewer. Directional.

Thumb rubs fingertips (MONEY) then moves off the palm towards whoever's being paid.

Hand flicks off the other hand, away from you. Directional.

Both thumbs tap, like typing on a phone.

How it works

Start = who does it, end = who receives it
  • I-GIVE-YOU: GIVE moves from your chest towards the other person.
  • YOU-GIVE-ME: GIVE starts near them and moves to you.
  • SHE-GIVE-HIM: set up two spots (Lesson 11), then GIVE moves from her spot to his.

No extra pronouns needed: the movement already says it. The Signbank videos show the neutral form; you change the direction.

Not every verb does this

Many verbs, like LIKE, KNOW and WANT, are made on the body and don't move between people. For those, you still use pointing pronouns: ME LIKE YOU.

Deaf culture: Directional verbs and sign names

This kind of spatial grammar is one of the things that makes signed languages full languages, not gestures or English on the hands. Research on Auslan (including the Auslan Corpus behind Signbank) has documented this in detail.

Put it together

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror and R to record a take, then compare it side by side with the reference.

  1. Flip it. I-GIVE-YOU, then YOU-GIVE-ME. Record and check the direction is obvious.
  2. Two spots. Set up MOTHER (left) and SISTER (right). Sign MOTHER-GIVE-SISTER, then SISTER-ASK-MOTHER.
  3. All verbs. Do every verb in both directions: me→you and you→me.
  4. Mini story. Your friend asks you for help, you help them, they give you a coffee.
  5. Record the story and check every verb moves the right way.

Check yourself

Recap

Directional verbs move from the doer to the receiver. Use real people or your set-up spots as the start and end points.
