Work & study

'What do you do?' is a common question. Learn jobs and study, and an important one for working with Deaf people: INTERPRETER.

By the end you can…

  • Ask and answer WHAT YOUR JOB?
  • Sign common jobs and places of study
  • Know what an Auslan interpreter does
  • Talk about your own work or study

2 · Study

Bent fingers wiggle above the other palm: reading and studying.

Hand from the forehead (LEARN) to the other palm.

3 · Interpreters

Both thumb-and-finger rings swap back and forth: translating between two languages.
Deaf culture: Interpreters

Auslan interpreters are trained professionals who interpret between Auslan and English, accredited through NAATI in Australia. They're bound by a code of ethics: confidential, impartial, and they interpret everything. As a learner, never act as someone's interpreter. Your role is to be a conversation partner. Deaf people can access interpreters for work, medical appointments and more, often funded through programs like the NDIS or JobAccess.

Job = person + WORK

For many jobs without their own sign, Deaf people fingerspell the job or describe it: ME WORK COMPUTER (I work in IT), ME WORK HOSPITAL.

Put it together

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror and R to record a take, then compare it side by side with the reference.

  1. Jobs. All the job signs, then fingerspell your own job if it has no sign here.
  2. Your work. ME WORK … (where, what with, how long: HOUR …).
  3. Ask. YOUR WORK WHAT? · YOU WORK WHERE? · YOU STUDY WHAT?
  4. Family jobs. Use space: place two family members and say what each does.
  5. Record a 10 s introduction about your work or study.

Check yourself

Recap

Ask YOUR WORK WHAT?. Describe jobs with WORK + place or thing. Interpreters are professionals; as a learner, be a conversation partner, not an interpreter.
