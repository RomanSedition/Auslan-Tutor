Depicting signs 1: people & vehicles

Depicting signs (also called classifiers) are where Auslan becomes like film. A handshape stands for a type of thing (an upright index finger is a person, a flat hand is a vehicle), and you move it to show exactly what happened.

By the end you can…

  • Use an upright index finger to represent a person
  • Use a flat hand to represent a vehicle
  • Show passing, following and crashing
  • Describe a simple scene with depicting signs

1 · People moving

Two upright index fingers (two people) move past each other.

One index finger follows behind the other hand.
The person handshape

An upright index finger stands for a person. Two fingers = two people. Move them to show what they do: walk towards each other (MEET, Lesson 2, works like this), pass by, walk away. The direction the finger faces can show which way the person is facing.

2 · Vehicles moving

Two flat hands (vehicles) slide past each other.

Two flat hands (vehicles) collide.
The vehicle handshape

A flat hand, palm down stands for a vehicle: a car, bus, bike. Move it to show the path: turning, speeding up, stopping, overtaking, crashing. First sign the noun (CAR), then use the depicting hand to show what it did.

Name it, then show it

The pattern is: sign the thing first (CAR, MAN, BIKE), then switch to the depicting handshape and move it. Without naming it first, people won't know what your hand represents.

Deaf culture: Visual storytelling

Deaf storytellers are famous for vivid, cinematic signing: zooming in, slowing down, showing multiple things at once. Depicting signs are the core of it. You'll see this at Deaf events and in Auslan stories online.

Put it together

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror and R to record a take, then compare it side by side with the reference.

  1. People. Two index fingers: walk towards each other, meet, then walk away.
  2. Vehicles. Flat hand: drive straight, turn left, stop, reverse.
  3. Name it first. CAR → (flat hand) drives past. MAN → (index) walks across.
  4. A scene. A car drives along, a person crosses, the car stops. Show it with both hands.
  5. Record and watch back: is it clear what each hand is?

Check yourself

Recap

Name it, then show it. Upright index = person; flat hand = vehicle. Move them to show paths, passing, following and crashing.
