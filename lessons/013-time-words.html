Time words & time-first order

Welcome to Unit 3. Auslan verbs don't change form for past or future the way English verbs do (walk / walked / will walk). Instead, you set the time at the start of the sentence, and everything after it happens then. You'll also meet the time line: the future is in front of you, the past behind.

By the end you can…

  • Sign the main time words: TODAY, TOMORROW, YESTERDAY, BEFORE, AFTER, LATER, SOON
  • Put the time first: YESTERDAY ME WORK
  • Use FINISH to show something already happened
  • Use the time line: forward = future, back = past

1 · Days

Australia-wide: index finger held up, small downward movement (also means NOW). Northern: flat hands at the chest.

Both open hands, palms up, drop slightly in front of you: 'right now, currently'.

Index finger at the cheek, moving forward: the future is in front of you.

Southern: index finger at the cheek/chin, moving back over the shoulder. Northern: hand at the chin moves back.
The time line

Notice the direction: TOMORROW moves forward, YESTERDAY moves back over the shoulder, and BEFORE moves back too. In Auslan the future is in front of your body and the past is behind it. Bigger or further movements mean further away in time.

2 · Order of events

Flat hand, palm back, moves back towards the shoulder: the past is behind you.

Southern: thumb-up hand moves forward from the other hand. Northern: index flicks forward. Watch at 0.5×.

Index finger moves forward off the other palm: 'later on'.

Thumb tip at the chin, then moves down and forward a little.

Open hands, palms facing you, flip out and down: 'done, finished'. Also marks the past.

3 · Weeks

Index finger moves forward along the other hand: one week ahead.

Southern: index at the cheek, then moves back along the other hand: one week behind.

How it works

Time first

Start with the time, then the rest: TOMORROW ME WORK (I'm working tomorrow), YESTERDAY ME SICK (I was sick yesterday). Once you've set the time, it stays until you change it, so you don't need to repeat it in every sentence.

FINISH marks the past

FINISH after a verb (or before it) means the action is done: ME EAT FINISH = I've eaten. It's one of the most common signs in everyday Auslan.

Deaf culture: Watch for the time sign

When watching Deaf people sign, look for the time sign at the start. If you miss it, you might think a story is happening now when it happened years ago. AGAIN PLEASE (Lesson 5) is your friend.

Put it together

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror and R to record a take, then compare it side by side with the reference.

  1. Time line. Sign YESTERDAY, TODAY, TOMORROW in a row. Check that back, here and forward are clear.
  2. Time first. Make three sentences: TODAY ME …, YESTERDAY ME …, TOMORROW ME … (use signs you know: TIRED, HAPPY, LEARN).
  3. FINISH. Sign ME LEARN FINGERSPELL FINISH, then YOU FINISH? with eyebrows up.
  4. Next and last week. Record NEXT-WEEK and LAST-WEEK and compare the direction of movement.
  5. Record a 10 s mini-diary: YESTERDAY … TODAY … TOMORROW …

Recap

Time goes first, then the rest of the sentence. The future is in front of you and the past behind. FINISH shows something's already happened.
