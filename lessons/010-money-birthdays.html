2 · Money

Thumb rubs across the fingertips: the 'money' gesture.

Fingers brush across the upturned palm.

Fingers flutter in front of the chin; eyebrows down.

Hand moves forward off the other palm, as if paying.
Prices

Number + DOLLAR: FIVE DOLLAR, FIFTEEN DOLLAR. HOW-MUCH? takes the eyebrows-down face. In real life, people also write prices down or show them on a phone.

3 · Years and birthdays

Index circles around the other hand: one trip around the sun.

Five regional versions! Southern: both hands raised, palms out, shaking. WA: from the mouth, then the index points forward.

BIRTHDAY has five versions on Signbank: Southern, Northern, Western Australian and two from Queensland. Your region's is shown first; click through the chips to see them all.

Deaf culture: Regional signs are a feature, not a problem

Auslan grew from different Deaf schools in different states, and some of that history survives in signs like BIRTHDAY and the colours. Deaf people from different states understand each other fine, and often enjoy comparing versions. Using your local sign shows you've learned from your local community.

Put it together

Mirror practice

Press M for the mirror and R to record a take, then compare it side by side with the reference.

  1. Prices. Sign five prices using 1–19 + DOLLAR, then add HUNDRED: TWO HUNDRED DOLLAR.
  2. At the shop. HOW-MUCH? → FIFTEEN DOLLAR → ME BUY. Then the reverse role.
  3. Birthdays. Ask YOUR BIRTHDAY WHEN? and click through all five BIRTHDAY versions, signing each.
  4. Your local sign. Record your region's BIRTHDAY and compare at 0.5×.
  5. Mix in Unit 1–2. MY MOTHER BIRTHDAY · ME BUY … FOR (point) SISTER.
