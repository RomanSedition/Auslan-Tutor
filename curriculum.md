# Auslan curriculum: 30-lesson beginner track

A beginner path from zero to holding simple everyday conversations and telling short stories.
Claude follows this order for "next lesson", adapts it to what's asked for, and ticks items off
as lessons are written.

## Pacing guide

| | Recommended |
|---|---|
| **Lessons per week** | **2** (3 if you're keen, 1 if busy: consistency matters more than speed) |
| **Time per lesson** | 30–40 min the first time through |
| **Daily practice** | 10 min: Review deck plus the fingerspelling game, on non-lesson days too |
| **New signs per lesson** | ~10–15 (review lessons: ~5) |
| **Whole track** | about 15 weeks at 2/week (≈ 350–400 signs) |

**When to move on:** when most of the previous lesson's signs are rated *Good* in Review and you can do its
mirror-practice steps without peeking. If the Review deck has lots of *Again*, take a review day instead
of a new lesson. Signs stick through spaced repetition, not cramming.

**Every 6th lesson is a review lesson.** It introduces few new signs, recycles everything from the unit
in longer conversations, and includes a recorded "can-do" check against the unit goals.

**Reality check:** this app teaches vocabulary and grammar, but conversation is a skill you only build
with people. From around lesson 12, try a Deaf community event, an Auslan meetup, or a class with a Deaf
teacher (TAFE and Deaf societies run Certificate II/III in Auslan courses).

## Lesson design rules
- Reuse 3–5 signs from earlier lessons in each lesson's phrases and quiz (built-in spaced review).
- Every lesson teaches non-manual features (face, head, body) alongside the hands.
- Regional signs (colours, for example) are added as variants so each learner sees their own region's
  version first. Check every sign's Signbank region tags; don't assume.
- Fingerspelling practice continues every lesson (names, places, words without signs).

## Unit 1: Foundations (Lessons 1–6)
*Can-do: introduce yourself, spell your name, give numbers, ask basic questions, and manage when you don't understand.*

- [x] **1. Fingerspelling: the two-handed alphabet.** Vowels, palm letters, spelling your name, reading short words.
- [x] **2. Greetings & introductions.** HELLO, HOW-ARE-YOU, NAME, WHAT, NICE MEET, DEAF/HEARING, LEARN AUSLAN; eyebrow grammar for questions.
- [x] **3. Numbers 0–19.** Number handshapes (the thumb pattern for 6–9), ages, phone numbers. (Signbank has no entry for 20.)
- [x] **4. Questions & facial expression.** WHO, WHERE, WHEN, WHY, HOW, WHICH, HOW-MANY, YES, NO. Wh-question face (brows down) vs yes/no face (brows up), in depth.
- [x] **5. Communication strategies.** AGAIN, SLOW, UNDERSTAND / NOT-UNDERSTAND, SORRY, PLEASE, EXCUSE-ME, WAIT, FINGERSPELL. Getting attention and turn-taking. Asking "how do you sign X?".
- [x] **6. ★ Review: meeting someone new.** A full first-meeting conversation using Unit 1. Recorded can-do check.

## Unit 2: People (Lessons 7–12)
*Can-do: talk about your family and friends, describe people, and say how you feel.*

- [x] **7. Family.** MOTHER, FATHER, SISTER, BROTHER, PARTNER, CHILD, GRANDPARENT; HAVE / NOT-HAVE; HOW-MANY BROTHER?
- [x] **8. Feelings & emotions.** HAPPY, SAD, TIRED, ANGRY, EXCITED, WORRIED, SICK, BORED. Showing intensity with the face and movement (a bit tired vs exhausted).
- [x] **9. Describing people & colours.** TALL, SHORT, HAIR, GLASSES, BEARD, OLD, YOUNG; colours (several have regional variants). Describing someone so another person can spot them.
- [x] **10. Big numbers, money & birthdays.** HUNDRED, THOUSAND, DOLLAR, MONEY, HOW-MUCH, BUY, YEAR, BIRTHDAY (five regional versions). Signbank has no entries for the tens (20, 30…), so revisit when they're added.
- [x] **11. Pronouns & signing space.** HE/SHE/THEY by pointing to set-up locations, WE, TWO-OF-US, plurals, MY/YOUR/HIS/THEIR. Placing people in space and referring back to them.
- [x] **12. ★ Review: my family & friends.** Introduce your family using signing space. Recorded can-do check.

## Unit 3: Time & daily life (Lessons 13–18)
*Can-do: describe your day and week, talk about food, and say what you do and don't do.*

- [x] **13. Time words & time-first word order.** TODAY, TOMORROW, YESTERDAY, NOW, LATER, BEFORE, FINISH (past). Time goes at the start of the sentence. The time line in space.
- [x] **14. Days, weeks, months & clock time.** Days of the week, WEEK, MONTH, YEAR, NEXT-WEEK, LAST-WEEK, telling the time, WHAT-TIME?
- [x] **15. Daily routine.** WAKE-UP, SHOWER, EAT, DRINK, WORK, GO, COME, HOME, SLEEP, EVERY-DAY, MORNING, AFTERNOON, NIGHT.
- [x] **16. Food & drink.** COFFEE, TEA, WATER, BREAD, MEAT, FRUIT, HUNGRY, THIRSTY, LIKE, WANT; ordering at a café.
- [x] **17. Negation.** Headshake, NOT, NOTHING, NEVER, CAN'T, DON'T-WANT, DON'T-LIKE, NOT-YET, DON'T-KNOW.
- [x] **18. ★ Review: my day and my week.** Describe a typical day and your plans for the week. Recorded can-do check.

## Unit 4: Out and about (Lessons 19–24)
*Can-do: talk about places, getting around, the weather, your interests and your work, and make plans.*

- [x] **19. Places around town.** HOME, SHOP, SCHOOL, HOSPITAL, BEACH, PARK, CITY, LIVE, WHERE-LIVE?; Australian places, fingerspelled or signed.
- [x] **20. Transport & directions.** CAR, BUS, TRAIN, BIKE, WALK, DRIVE, LEFT, RIGHT, STRAIGHT, NEAR, FAR. Using space and pointing to give directions.
- [x] **21. Weather & seasons.** HOT, COLD, RAIN, SUN, WIND, STORM, SUMMER, WINTER; talking about the weekend weather.
- [x] **22. Hobbies & sport.** SWIM, FOOTBALL, CRICKET, READ, MOVIE, MUSIC, COOK, TRAVEL; LIKE, FAVOURITE, PREFER.
- [x] **23. Work & study.** WORK, JOB, STUDY, TEACHER, DOCTOR, STUDENT, OFFICE, BOSS; WHAT YOUR JOB?; access and interpreters at work.
- [x] **24. ★ Review: making plans with a friend.** Arrange a weekend outing: when, where, how to get there. Recorded can-do check.

## Unit 5: Grammar in space & storytelling (Lessons 25–30)
*Can-do: use space, directional verbs and depicting signs to tell a short story about something that happened.*

- [x] **25. Directional verbs.** GIVE, ASK, TELL, HELP, SHOW, PAY, SEND: the movement shows who does what to whom (I-GIVE-YOU vs YOU-GIVE-ME).
- [x] **26. Depicting signs 1: people & vehicles moving.** Classifier handshapes for a person, a car or a bike; showing paths, speed and position (the car drove past, two people met).
- [x] **27. Depicting signs 2: size & shape.** Tracing and size-and-shape signs: describing objects, rooms and layouts (a round table, a long thin road).
- [x] **28. Role shift: becoming the character.** Shifting your body and eye gaze to quote people and show their reactions. Constructed action.
- [x] **29. Deaf culture & community.** Deaf community history and identity, Auslan's status, Deaf schools and clubs, interpreters, technology (captions, relay services, video calls), etiquette as a hearing learner. Signs for these topics.
- [x] **30. ★ Final review: tell a story.** Sign a short story about your weekend or a memorable event, using time, space, directional verbs, depicting signs and role shift. Record it, then compare with your Lesson 2 recordings. Where to go next.
